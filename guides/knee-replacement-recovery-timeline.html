Knee Replacement Recovery: A Week-by-Week Timeline

From day one to month six — what's normal, what's ahead of schedule, and the two numbers that predict your outcome.

Home › Guides › Knee Replacement Recovery: A Week-by-Week Timeline

Published Jan 2027 · Reviewed against current Michigan law and payer policy · General information, not medical or legal advice

Quick answer: most people walk with support the day of surgery, drive around weeks 4–6, feel 'mostly themselves' by month 3, and keep improving to a year. The two numbers that matter most: knee extension (getting it fully straight) and flexion past ~110–120° — hit those early and nearly everything else follows.

Weeks 0–2: protect, move, straighten

Walking short distances with a walker, managing swelling, and — the part people underestimate — working extension hard. A knee that heals slightly bent stays bent; we fight for full straightening from day one. Expect therapy 2–3×/week starting within days of surgery.

Weeks 2–6: bend, strengthen, normalize walking

Flexion climbs toward 110–120°, the walker gives way to a cane and then nothing, and quad strengthening begins in earnest. Driving usually returns around weeks 4–6 (right knee later than left for most vehicles) once you're off narcotics and can brake decisively.

Weeks 6–12: build the engine

Stairs step-over-step, longer walks, real strength work. Night aching and stiffness after sitting are still normal here — the implant is fine; the tissues are remodeling.

Months 3–12: the payoff

By month 3 most daily life is back; strength and confidence keep compounding to a year. Our patients re-test on instrumented strength measurement at discharge so 'recovered' is a number, not a feeling.

Ahead of the curve, or behind it?

Timelines are averages. What matters is trend: motion improving weekly, strength climbing, swelling trending down. If you're stalled two weeks running, the plan changes — more of the same is not a plan. Scheduled for surgery? Pre-op strengthening ('prehab') measurably improves outcomes, and booking post-op slots in advance means zero gap after clearance. Medicare covers both, with no hard visit cap.
