How we treat hip pain

Hip problems hide behind each other: arthritis, bursitis-pattern lateral pain, labral irritation, and referred pain from the back can all feel similar. Our evaluation separates them with movement and strength testing, then treats the real driver — progressive hip and core strengthening, manual therapy for stiffness, and gait retraining so improvement survives the walk to your car.

Signs this page is about you

  • Groin or front-of-hip pain with walking or stairs
  • Aching on the outside of the hip, worse lying on that side
  • Stiffness putting on shoes and socks
  • Hip pain that arrived after back trouble
Michigan direct access: most patients can start physical therapy without a physician referral (10 visits / 21 days before a prescription is needed; wellness services exempt). Insurance plan rules vary — we verify yours and obtain any required referral before visit one.

Common questions

Can physical therapy help hip arthritis without surgery?

Yes — structured strengthening and mobility work reliably reduces arthritic hip pain and often delays or avoids replacement. And if replacement does come, entering it stronger predicts a faster recovery.

Why does my hip hurt more at night?

Side-lying compresses the lateral hip tissues (the gluteal tendons and bursa). Load management plus targeted strengthening usually settles night pain within a few weeks.

Is my hip pain actually coming from my back?

Sometimes — the two refer pain to each other. Our first-visit screen tests both so you treat the right joint from day one.
